# UK Tax Computation Using Microsoft Excel

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates the preparation of UK individual and company tax computations using Microsoft Excel. It focuses on reconciling accounting profit to taxable profit, identifying tax adjustments, calculating capital allowances, and determining tax liabilities using a fictional individual and company dataset.

The project is designed to develop practical skills relevant to entry-level tax, audit, and accounting roles.

## Objectives

- Prepare an individual income tax computation for a self-employed taxpayer.
- Prepare a company Corporation Tax computation.
- Identify allowable and disallowable expenses for tax purposes.
- Reconcile accounting profit to adjusted trading profit and taxable total profits.
- Calculate capital allowances using the Annual Investment Allowance (AIA).
- Apply Corporation Tax rates and marginal relief assumptions.
- Organise tax calculations and supporting workings in Excel.

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- UK tax computation principles
- HMRC tax rates and allowances as applicable to the stated tax year

## Key Areas Covered

### 1. Individual Tax Computation

The individual computation starts with accounting profit and adjusts for items such as:

- Accounting depreciation
- Client entertaining
- Fines and penalties
- Personal expenditure
- Capital allowances

The adjusted trading profit is then used to calculate taxable non-savings income after the applicable Personal Allowance.

### 2. Company Tax Computation

The company computation includes:

- Profit before tax
- Add-back of accounting depreciation
- Disallowable client entertaining
- Capital-related legal fees
- Deduction for qualifying capital allowances
- Corporation Tax calculation
- Marginal relief working, subject to applicable conditions

### 3. Tax Reconciliation

The reconciliation demonstrates the relationship between accounting profit, tax adjustments, and the resulting adjusted or taxable profit.

### 4. Capital Allowances

A separate working records qualifying equipment expenditure and the assumed Annual Investment Allowance claim.

## Assumptions and Limitations

- All individual and company information is fictional.
- The workbook is an educational portfolio exercise, not professional tax advice.
- Tax rates and thresholds depend on the relevant tax year and jurisdiction.
- The calculations use simplified assumptions and may not cover every statutory adjustment, relief, restriction, or filing requirement.
- The Corporation Tax marginal relief calculation assumes no associated companies or other relevant adjustments affecting the applicable limits or augmented profits.
- The calculations should be reviewed against official HMRC guidance before use in a real tax return or client engagement.
